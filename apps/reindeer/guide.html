Reindeer jump game: how to play and run further across the rooftops

Updated November 18, 2026

Reindeer Jump is an endless runner you play with a single button. A reindeer races across snowy rooftops on a winter night, and your only job is deciding when to jump and how high. Gaps between buildings, chimneys, snow piles, ice and the odd crow stand in the way, while presents and rare golden bells wait to be collected. The run speeds up the longer you survive, so every metre counts. This guide explains the controls, what each obstacle does, habits that lead to longer runs and a little background on real reindeer and on runner games.

How to play

Press the start button and the reindeer appears on the first rooftop. Nothing moves until your first touch or key press, so you can get comfortable before the run begins. That first press only starts the run; from then on every new press is a jump. The reindeer runs to the right by itself, and the rooftops scroll towards you.

There is one button, but it does three things. A quick tap gives a short hop. Holding the button while the reindeer rises makes the jump much higher and longer, and letting go early cuts it short. While you are in the air you can press once more for a double jump, which gives an extra lift. On a phone or tablet, tap anywhere on the game area. On a computer, use the Space bar, the up arrow, W, or click with the mouse.

  • Tap = short hop, hold = high and long jump.
  • Press again in mid-air for one double jump; it comes back when you land.
  • Falling into a gap between two roofs ends the run at once.
  • Chimneys, snow piles, ice and crows each cost one of your three hearts.
  • Switching tabs or locking your phone pauses the run and the clock.

What you will meet on the rooftops

Gaps are the most important thing to watch. Buildings stand side by side with dark alleys between them, and if the reindeer runs off the edge without jumping it drops out of sight. A press right after you leave the edge still counts as a normal jump, and a press made just before landing is remembered and fires the moment your hooves touch the snow, so the controls forgive tiny timing slips. Chimneys are tall brick stacks with smoke curling from the top; the smoke is harmless, the bricks are not. Snow piles and blocks of ice are low and easy to clear with a short hop. Later in the run, crows begin to hover at head height, so even a flat stretch of roof can call for a jump.

Bumping into anything costs one heart. Right after a bump the reindeer blinks for a moment and cannot be hurt again, which gives you time to recover. Presents add ten points each and often sit on the open stretches between obstacles. Golden bells are worth fifty and usually float high above a chimney, a crow or a gap, where only a full, held jump reaches them. Every gap and obstacle is generated so that one held jump at the current speed clears it with room to land before the next hazard, so a clean path always exists.

Tips for longer runs

Most runs end at a gap, not at an obstacle, because a missed gap is instant while a bump only costs a heart. Treat gaps as the priority: when a gap and a present compete for your attention, take care of the gap. As the pace rises, the moment to jump comes earlier, so watch a little further ahead instead of at the reindeer's feet.

  • Hold the button for gaps and tall chimneys; tap for snow, ice and crows.
  • Save the double jump for rescues instead of using it on every jump.
  • Release as soon as you clear a chimney so you land sooner and keep your rhythm.
  • Do not chase a bell if it means jumping late for the next gap.
  • If you lose a heart, use the blinking moment to reset your timing calmly.

Real reindeer and the history of runner games

Reindeer, known as caribou in North America, live across the Arctic and sub-Arctic. They are the only deer in which both males and females grow antlers, and their wide hooves work like snowshoes on deep snow and like shovels when they dig for lichen. Some reindeer can see ultraviolet light, which helps them spot food and predators against bright winter snow. Reindeer have been herded for centuries by peoples of northern Scandinavia and Siberia, and in winter stories they became the classic animal that pulls a sleigh through the night sky, which is why a reindeer on snowy rooftops feels at home in December.

Runner games grew out of early side-scrolling platformers, where the screen moved with the hero. Around 2009, small one-button games such as Canabalt showed that a character who never stops running, with the player only choosing when to jump, could be thrilling. Phones made the idea popular, since a tap is the most natural input on a touch screen, and many later runners added variable jump height and double jumps to give that single button more depth. Reindeer Jump follows the same tradition with short runs that are easy to start and hard to put down.

Playing with friends

Runner games are perfect for friendly score battles. Take turns on the same phone, or send the link and compare scores in a group chat. Try challenges such as finishing a run without using the double jump, or collecting as many bells as possible before the first lost heart. At the end of every run you can see how your score compares with other players' real results, and your personal best is saved in your browser so you can keep chasing it.
