Advent calendar maker: how to make 24 little surprises

Updated November 17, 2026

The Advent Calendar Maker lets you hide a short note behind each of 24 doors and send the whole calendar as a single link. From December 1 the person you made it for can open one door a day, right up to Christmas Eve. This guide covers where the tradition comes from, how to fill your calendar, what kinds of messages work well, how to make it special when you live far apart, and what happens to your messages.

A short history of advent calendars

Advent is the season of waiting before Christmas, and families in nineteenth-century Germany found simple ways to count it down. Some drew chalk lines on a door and wiped one away each day, others lit a candle or hung a small picture every morning. Children, as you can imagine, mostly wanted to know how many sleeps were left.

Printed calendars with little doors appeared in the early twentieth century, and after the Second World War versions with a piece of chocolate behind every door became a staple in shops across Europe and later the rest of the world. Today there are calendars filled with tea, toys, beauty samples and even socks. The idea underneath has not changed, though: a small, reliable moment of joy every day that makes the wait feel shorter.

How to make your calendar

Start by choosing one of four designs: a classic red and green, a snowy blue, a starry gold on a night sky, or a candy pink. Then type who the calendar is for and who it is from. Both are optional, but a name at the top makes the moment of opening feel personal.

Below that you will find 24 lines, one for every door. Each message can be up to 80 characters, which is about one or two short sentences. Write as many as you like. If a door stays empty, your friend still finds a little treat behind it, such as a cookie or a candy cane, so you never have to worry about blank days. When you run out of ideas, tap the dice next to any door for a suggestion, or fill every empty door at once and edit the ones you want to change.

  • Pick a design and add the names.
  • Write a short message for some or all of the 24 doors.
  • Use the dice for ideas, then edit them so they sound like you.
  • Tap Preview to check every message as your friend will see it.
  • Create the link, copy it and send it in your favourite chat app.

Ideas for 24 days of messages

Twenty-four messages sounds like a lot, but it gets easy when you mix a few kinds of notes. Think of the calendar as a playlist: a funny one, then a sweet one, then something your friend can actually do that day. Short and specific beats long and general. A shared memory with one detail will mean more than a paragraph of compliments.

Save something special for the last doors. Door 24 is Christmas Eve, so it is a good place for your warmest message or a hint about a real present. Weekends are a nice time for a small challenge or a plan, while busy weekdays call for something quick that makes your friend smile on the way to work or school.

  • Memories: a moment you both still laugh about.
  • Coupons: a promise such as a movie night, a hug or a chore you will do.
  • Tiny missions: a photo to take, a song to play, a walk to go on.
  • Compliments: one thing you honestly admire about them.
  • Countdown cheers: a nudge that Christmas is getting closer.

Tips for long-distance friends and couples

An advent calendar is a lovely way to stay close when you cannot be in the same city. Because the doors open by the date on your friend’s own phone, it works across time zones: they open door 5 on their December 5, wherever they are. You might agree to open your doors at the same moment on a video call, or send each other calendars and compare notes every evening.

Coupons work especially well at a distance when they point to the next time you meet, for example a dinner, a trip or a long walk together. You can also link a door to something that happens in real life, such as a letter in the post or a song you will send that day. Before you share, open the preview to make sure every message reads well in order.

When the doors open, and what happens to your messages

Door 1 unlocks on December 1, door 2 on December 2, and so on until door 24 on December 24. If your friend taps a door too early, it gives a little shake and shows how many days are left. Opened doors stay open on their device, and from December 25 until the end of January every door is open, so late starters can catch up.

Your messages are never stored on our server. Everything lives inside the link itself, which is why you cannot change a message after sending it; simply edit your calendar and send a new link instead. Anyone who has the link can read each message once its door unlocks, so share it only with the person it is for, and keep addresses or other private details out of your notes. The Save image button creates a picture of the closed calendar with door numbers only, so you can post it without giving any surprise away.
