Anonymous voting on one phone: how to run a fair secret ballot

Updated November 3, 2026

When a group has to decide something together, a show of hands is quick but rarely honest. People look around before raising an arm, nobody wants to be the only one voting against the boss, and the loudest friend usually wins. A secret ballot fixes that. This guide explains how to run an anonymous vote with a single phone, why secret ballots give better answers, how to host a fun year-end award night, and how the app settles a tie without anyone feeling cheated.

How to run a secret vote on one phone

Start by choosing a template or writing your own questions. You can ask up to five questions in one round, each with two to twelve choices. For awards, tick “vote for a person” and type the names once in the names list; every award question will then use the same list, so you never have to retype anyone. Finally set how many people will vote.

When you start, the screen hides everything and asks for voter 1. Hand the phone over. The voter taps the button when nobody else can see the screen, picks one choice in every question and casts the vote. The ballot disappears immediately and a “vote saved” screen tells them to pass the phone on. When everyone has voted, gather around for the reveal.

  • Pick a template or write up to five questions.
  • Add the names list if you are voting for people.
  • Set the number of voters and start.
  • Pass the phone: each person votes alone, then hands it on.
  • Reveal the results together, one question at a time.

Why anonymous voting is fairer

Open voting measures courage as much as opinion. Psychologists have shown for decades that people adjust their answers to match the group, especially when a respected or senior person has already spoken. In a secret ballot that pressure disappears: nobody can see your choice, so you can vote for the quiet colleague who did the most work, or admit that you would rather have pizza than another night of karaoke.

This app goes one step further than paper slips. It never stores who voted for what. Each ballot only adds one to the totals of the chosen options, and the screen is wiped before the next person takes the phone. During voting nobody, not even the organizer, sees a running tally, so early votes cannot sway later ones. If someone cannot come, the organizer can end voting early and reveal the results with the votes already cast.

Hosting a year-end award night

Awards are the highlight of many year-end parties, and a secret ballot keeps them fun instead of political. Choose three to five categories that celebrate people warmly: most valuable player, best mood maker, the person who surprised everyone, the best helper. Avoid categories that could embarrass someone, because the point is for everyone to leave the evening feeling appreciated.

Timing matters. Vote early in the evening while people are fresh, then save the reveal for after dinner. The app reveals one question at a time with a short drumroll, which gives the host a natural moment to build suspense, read the winner aloud and hand over a small prize.

  • Keep categories positive and inclusive.
  • Use the names list so every award shares the same nominees.
  • Let people vote privately in a quiet corner, not at the table.
  • Prepare small prizes before the reveal.
  • Copy the results as text to post in your group chat afterwards.

Ties and the fair tie-break draw

With a small group, ties happen often. When two or more choices share first place, the app shows the tie honestly instead of quietly picking one. You can then press the tie-break button, which draws one of the tied options using the browser’s cryptographic random generator. Each tied option has exactly the same chance, and the result is clearly labeled as a draw so nobody mistakes luck for a landslide.

You can also skip the draw and settle a tie your own way, for example with a quick second round between the tied options only, or by giving both winners the prize. Many groups enjoy a rule agreed before voting starts, such as “ties go to a draw”, so the decision feels fair to everyone.

Restaurants, classrooms and sharing the results

The same tool works for everyday decisions. Use a template to pick where to eat or what to watch on movie night, or write your own questions for a class trip, a club logo or the theme of the next party. Because everyone votes on one phone, it works anywhere without accounts, downloads or internet sign-ups.

After the reveal you can copy the results as text or share a result link. The link contains only the questions, the choices and the final totals, never who voted for what, so friends who missed the evening can see the outcome and even start their own vote. For remote groups, keep in mind that a pass-the-phone ballot only stays secret when each person holds the phone alone. Run the vote at your next meet-up, or let each team in a different city vote on its own phone and add up the shared result links afterwards.
