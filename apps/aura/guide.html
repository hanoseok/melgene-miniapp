Aura Color Test: what color is your aura and how does the quiz work?

Updated October 9, 2026

The aura color test is a free, two-minute quiz that turns twelve everyday questions into a color and a short personality portrait. This guide explains what an aura is supposed to be, how the quiz works, what colors can and cannot say about you, and how to use the result for fun and reflection.

What an aura is, and what this quiz does

In popular spirituality, an aura is a glow of energy said to surround a person, with each color linked to a mood, a temperament or a way of relating to others. The idea is old and appears in many traditions, and it became familiar in the modern West through twentieth-century spiritual writing. It is worth being clear about one thing from the start: scientists have not found any measurable energy field around people that matches these descriptions. Photographs that appear to show a glow around a hand, such as Kirlian photography developed in the 1930s, are explained by electrical effects and moisture, not by a personal energy.

So why use an aura quiz at all? Because the colors make a beautiful, memorable language for talking about temperament. This quiz asks twelve questions about ordinary moments, each with four answers, and adds up your choices to find the color whose personality description sits closest to your pattern. Think of it as a mood board for your character, not a reading of anything physical. Your result includes a short portrait, a few strengths, how other people may see you, a best-matching aura and a clashing one.

How to take the aura test

You need about two minutes and no account. A little preparation helps you get a result that feels like you.

There is no right or wrong answer, and nothing is scored as good or bad. Every color has its own strengths, so you have no reason to steer toward the one that sounds most impressive.

  • Find a quiet moment and take a breath. A calm mind answers more honestly than a rushed one.
  • Read each of the twelve questions and picture a real recent situation instead of an ideal one.
  • Pick the answer you would genuinely choose today, not the one you think you should choose.
  • Use the back arrow if you want to change an earlier answer; nothing is final until the last question.
  • After the last answer, wait a moment for the result page with your color, strengths, how others see you, and your best and clashing matches.

Colors, moods and what research actually says

Color psychology is popular, but its claims need care. Studies show that colors can influence mood and perception a little, and that the effect depends heavily on context, culture and personal history. White is associated with weddings in many countries and with mourning in parts of East Asia, and a color that feels calming to you may feel dull to your neighbor. Anyone who tells you a particular color definitively predicts your personality is overstating the evidence.

The same caution applies to the feeling that your result is uncannily accurate. That feeling is partly the Barnum or Forer effect: in 1949 the psychologist Bertram Forer gave students a supposedly personal description that was really the same for everyone, and most of them rated it as a strong match. Warm, flattering and fairly general statements fit almost anyone. The healthy way to use an aura result is to ask yourself which parts ring true and which do not, rather than to accept the whole thing as fact.

Using your result with friends and for yourself

An aura result is a good conversation starter. Take it together with friends or a partner and compare colors, then discuss where the descriptions feel right and where they miss. The best-match and clashing sections are easy to treat as playful jokes: they are an invitation to think about which kinds of people balance you out and which kinds challenge you, not a prediction about compatibility.

Your answers depend on how you feel right now, so your color can change with your mood, a stressful week or a new chapter in life. Retake the quiz in a month, or after a big change, and notice what shifts. Some people write the result in a journal and add a line about what they would like to bring out more. That turns a five-minute quiz into a small self-reflection exercise. When you share the result with the share button, friends who open the link can take the same quiz and see their own color.

For entertainment only, and your privacy

This test is for entertainment and self-reflection. It is not a psychological assessment, it does not measure any real energy, and it cannot diagnose or predict anything. Please do not use it for important decisions about work, health or relationships, and if something in your life is worrying you, talk to a qualified professional.

About privacy: the quiz needs no sign-up or personal details. Your answers are scored inside your browser and are not stored on our servers. We only count, anonymously, which color came up so we can show how common each result is. Cookies and advertising are explained in the privacy policy at the bottom of the page.
