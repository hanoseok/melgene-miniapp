Christmas card maker: how to make a card people keep

Updated November 1, 2026

The Christmas Card Maker turns a design, a greeting and a few honest words into a finished card that you can save as a picture or send as a link that opens like a real envelope. This guide walks through how the tool works, how to write a message that feels personal instead of generic, where the Christmas card tradition comes from, and a few ideas for sharing your card this season.

How to make your card

Start by choosing one of six designs: a classic red card, a snowy night, a golden one with soft rays of light, a cute pastel card, a calm minimal one, or a winter forest. Then pick a greeting. Besides Merry Christmas you can choose Happy Holidays, Happy New Year or Season’s Greetings, which is handy when you are writing to friends who celebrate differently or when the card will arrive closer to New Year.

Next, switch the decorations on or off. Snow scatters flakes around the edges, lights hang a string of coloured bulbs across the top, and sparkle adds small stars in the corners. Finally, type who the card is for, who it is from and a message of up to 120 characters. The preview redraws as you type, so you can see straight away whether a line wraps nicely or needs trimming.

  • Pick a design that suits the person, not just the season.
  • Choose a greeting and the decorations you like.
  • Add the names and a short message, or tap Ideas for a starting point.
  • Check the preview, then tap Finish my card.
  • Save the image, copy the text or share the link.

Writing a heartfelt message

The best card messages are specific. “Merry Christmas, hope you’re well” is kind, but “Thank you for the soup when I was sick in March” is the line people remember. Think of one moment from the year you shared with this person, or one thing they did that mattered, and mention it in a single sentence. Then add a wish that looks forward: a calm holiday, a visit you are planning, or simply good things in the year ahead.

Keep the tone close to how you really talk. A card to a grandparent can be warm and simple, a card to a colleague can be friendly without being too personal, and a card to a close friend can include an inside joke. If you are stuck, the Ideas button fills in a few warm sentences written for your language; use one as it is or change a word or two so it sounds like you. Short is fine. A card is not a letter, and two honest lines usually beat five polite ones.

A short history of Christmas cards

The first commercial Christmas card is usually traced to London in 1843, when Sir Henry Cole asked the artist John Callcott Horsley to design a printed card so he would not have to write so many letters by hand. It showed a family raising a toast and carried the words “A Merry Christmas and a Happy New Year to You”. Cheaper printing and the new penny post soon made sending cards a popular habit across Britain.

In the following decades the custom spread to the United States and much of Europe, with robins, snowy villages, holly and Christmas trees becoming favourite motifs. In many countries the greeting shifted with local traditions: in Russia, for example, New Year is the big winter celebration, so cards there often wish a happy New Year first. Today digital cards carry the same idea a little faster: a picture and a few words that show someone they are on your mind.

Sending and sharing your card

When you finish, you have three ways to send it. Save image creates a 1080×1350 picture that fits nicely in chat apps and stories; on a phone it opens the share sheet, so you can send it straight to a conversation. Copy text copies the greeting, names, message and link so you can paste them into an email or message. The share buttons send the link itself.

The person who opens your link first sees a sealed envelope. One tap opens it and the card appears, with the greeting shown in their own language. If they have reduced motion turned on in their device settings, the card simply opens without the animation. They can then make a card of their own and send one back.

Ideas for using cards this season

Cards are not only for family. Send one to a teacher, a neighbour who helped with a parcel, or a friend you have not seen in a while. Make a single card for a group chat, print the saved image to tuck into a gift, or use the minimal design as a quiet thank-you note at work.

Your message stays private in the sense that it is never stored on our server: it lives only inside the link and, while you are editing, in your own browser. Because anyone who has the link can read the card, share it only with the people you mean, and leave out addresses or phone numbers.
