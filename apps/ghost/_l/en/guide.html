Ghost Maker: how to design a cute Halloween ghost

Updated October 9, 2026

Ghost Maker is a free dress-up tool for one small, friendly ghost. You choose its body, colour, face, hat and scene, give it a name, and it floats on your screen. This guide explains how the editor works, how to design a ghost with real personality, where ghost costumes come from, and what you can do with the finished picture.

How Ghost Maker works

The editor is built around eight groups of parts: body shape, colour, eyes, mouth, cheeks, hat or decoration, something to hold, and a background scene. Tabs along the top switch between the groups, and each tab shows a row of options. Tap one and your ghost changes instantly in the preview. A shortcut worth knowing: tapping the ghost itself jumps to the next option in the tab you are on, which is the quickest way to flip through every eye style without scrolling.

With that many choices the number of possible ghosts adds up fast. Multiply the options in each group together and you get more than two million different combinations, before you even type a name. The Random button shuffles everything at once, which is a handy way to find a combination you would never have chosen on purpose.

  • Open the editor and pick a tab such as Body, Eyes or Hat.
  • Tap an option, or tap the ghost to cycle to the next one.
  • Optionally type a name of up to 20 characters.
  • Press Random for a surprise, then fine-tune the parts you like.
  • Tap All done to see your finished, floating ghost.

Design tips for a ghost with personality

Start with the eyes and mouth, because the face carries almost all of a character’s personality. Wide eyes with a tiny mouth read as shy or startled; half-closed eyes with a wide smile look sleepy and relaxed. Mismatched moods are fun too: a grumpy mouth under sparkling eyes gives you a ghost who is trying to be scary and failing.

Next, think about contrast. A pale ghost on a pale scene almost disappears, while a dark scene makes light colours glow. Colour sets the mood as well: soft pink or lavender feels gentle, a deep midnight shade looks mysterious, and a glowing green looks like it escaped from a haunted house. Finish with the small details. A hat gives your ghost a job or a hobby, and the object it holds tells a story in a single picture. A short, sound-effect style name such as Little Boo is easy to remember and suits the character.

Where ghost costumes come from

Many historians connect Halloween with Samhain, an old autumn festival of the Gaelic world that marked the end of the harvest and the turn toward winter. In those traditions the boundary between the living and the dead was thought to grow thin at that time of year, and people lit fires and wore disguises. The Christian feast of All Hallows’ Eve, the evening before All Saints’ Day on 1 November, later gave Halloween its name.

The classic sheet ghost stays popular because anyone can make one in a minute: a white shape and two dark eye holes are enough for people to recognise it at once. In picture books, cartoons and party decorations, ghosts have gradually become friendlier characters, and Ghost Maker leans into that gentle side of the tradition.

Ways to use your ghost

A finished ghost is a small piece of character art, and there are plenty of places it can live. Because the editor is quick, it also works well as a group game: give everyone one minute and one theme, such as sleepiest ghost or fanciest ghost, and compare the results.

  • Party decoration: show several ghosts with different moods and let guests guess who made which.
  • Profile picture or chat sticker for the Halloween season.
  • Classroom or family activity: everyone designs a ghost, then describes it as a character in a short story.
  • A friendly greeting: send a ghost with a name that matches the person you are writing to.
  • Drawing practice: use your design as a reference and redraw the ghost by hand.

Saving, sharing and privacy

Save image turns your ghost into a still PNG picture. On a phone it opens the share sheet so you can keep the picture in your photos or send it straight to a chat; on a computer it downloads as a file. The floating animation is only for the screen, and the saved picture is always still. If your device is set to reduce motion, the ghost stays still on screen too.

The share link contains the whole design, including the name, so a friend who opens it sees exactly your ghost and can then make their own. Your design is not uploaded to our servers. Because the name travels inside the link, avoid typing personal details such as a full name or an address. Ghost Maker is free and runs in your browser without sign-up.
